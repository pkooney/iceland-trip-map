Recommended Self-Guided Walking Tour (1 Hour) This 0.8-mile (1.3 km) flat loop starts and ends at the main parking area. It follows clearly defined, roped gravel pathways and boardwalks to keep you safely away from the boiling water. Stop 1: The Main Gates & Littli-Geysir: Enter from the parking lot and follow the steam. You will immediately pass Littli-Geysir, a small, vigorously bubbling churn of muddy water that serves as an appetizer for the main vents. Stop 2: Strokkur Geyser (The Main Event): Walk 3 minutes further to the roped viewing ring around Strokkur. Stand upwind (watch where the steam is blowing) to avoid getting drenched by sulfurous mist when it erupts. Stay for 2–3 eruptions to catch the perfect photo of the bright blue bubble that forms right before the water explodes. Stop 3: Great Geysir: Walk 2 minutes past Strokkur to see the legendary Great Geysir. While it is the namesake for all geysers worldwide, it is currently dormant and rarely erupts. However, its massive, steaming blue crater is highly photogenic. Stop 4: Blesi Hot Springs: Continue slightly uphill behind Geysir to find Blesi. This unique feature consists of two adjacent pools separated by a narrow rock wall. One pool is boiling hot and crystal clear, while the other is a stunning, opaque turquoise blue caused by suspended silica. Stop 5: King's Stones (Konungssteinar): Loop back down toward the visitor center, passing three historic stones engraved with the initials of Danish kings who visited the site in 1874, 1898, and 1907.

Step 1: The Lower Boardwalk Path Action: Park in the lower parking lot if spaces are available to bypass the massive upper staircase. Step onto the wooden boardwalk. The Vibe: Walk the Sigríður pathway running right alongside the canyon edge. You will feel the roaring rumble of the river and get sprayed by the dense mist as the water plunges 105 feet into the rugged crevice below. Step 2: The Rock Ledge Point Action: Follow the boardwalk to its furthest safe terminus on the wide stone outcrop thrusting out over the drop-off. The Vibe: This is the absolute best vantage point to photograph the two distinct, angled tiers of the waterfall cascading in unison. Step 3: The Upper Canyon Viewpoint Action: Walk back and climb the wooden stairs up to the upper cliff edge trail. The Vibe: This higher angle gives you a sweeping, dramatic view of the entire Hvítá river valley stretching out toward the distant Langjökull glacier. Step 4: The Historic Memorial Stop Action: Stop briefly at the stone profile monument dedicated to Sigríður Tómasdóttir. The Vibe: Read the plaques honoring this historic local woman who famously walked barefoot to Reykjavík to legally fight off international developers, successfully saving Gullfoss from being turned into a hydroelectric dam in the early 1900s. Step 5: Warm Up at the Visitor Center Action: Conclude your loop at the large main visitor complex. The Vibe: Browse high-end Icelandic wool apparel and grab a warm cup of coffee or a bowl of traditional Icelandic lamb soup at the Gullfoss Café to recover from the cold misty spray.

Step-by-Step Tour Highlights 9:40 AM – 10:05 AM | Walk the Bridge Between Continents The Route: Depart Reykjavík along Route 41 and cut south on Route 44 into the barren sand plains. The Experience: Pull into the parking lot for the Bridge Between Continents. Walk across the small footbridge that spans a deep, sandy canyon fissure. This fissure marks the literal dividing line where the North American and Eurasian tectonic plates are drifting apart by an inch every year. Take a photo standing with one foot in Europe and one foot in America. 10:15 AM – 10:55 AM | Reykjanesviti Lighthouse & Valahnúkamöl Cliffs The Route: Cruise 10 minutes south on Route 425 toward the rugged tip of the peninsula. The Experience: Marvel at Reykjanesviti, which is the oldest standing lighthouse in Iceland, perched high on a green volcanic hill. Drive just past it to the coastline to view Valahnúkamöl. Walk the boulder-strewn ridge to look out at towering sea stacks crashing out of the wild Atlantic Ocean. If the horizon is clear, you can spot the remote volcanic island of Eldey sitting 9 miles out at sea. 11:00 AM – 11:25 AM | Witness the Power of Gunnuhver Hot Springs The Route: Drive 2 minutes inland along the gravel access track. The Experience: Step onto the wooden boardwalks surrounding Gunnuhver, the most powerful geothermal mud pool in Iceland. The vents release a constant roar of superheated, mineral-rich steam that blankets the neon-orange volcanic landscape. Note: The pool is named after "Gunna," a historical local ghost who folklore says was tricked and thrown into the boiling vents by a clever priest. 11:40 AM – 12:05 PM | The Brimketill Lava Rock Pool The Route: Turn east onto Route 427 along the southern volcanic coast. The Experience: Follow the viewing platform down to Brimketill. This looks like a carved, man-made infinity pool but is entirely carved out of black basalt rock by ocean erosion. When the tide is high, waves smash into the volcanic ledge, sending dramatic geysers of seawater shooting high into the air.
